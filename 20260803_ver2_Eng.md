## 3. Definitions of Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------|
| Stay | An activity record in which a user was determined to have stayed in the same building from the start time to the end time. |
| Co-stay | A state in which two or more different users stay in the same building and the same time division while satisfying the specified conditions. In this study, it represents a potential opportunity for contact rather than actual conversation or interaction. |
| Old method | A method in which one day is classified into six time periods using the midpoint time of each stay, and different users whose actual stay periods overlap by 5 minutes or more on the same day, in the same building, and in the same time period are determined to be co-staying. |
| New method | A method in which one day is divided into 144 slots of 10 minutes each, each stay is assigned when it overlaps with a slot by 8 minutes or more, and a co-stay is determined when two or more different users are present in the same building and the same slot. |
| Co-stay network | A network in which users are represented as nodes and co-stay relationships are represented as edges. The number of co-stays or co-stay duration can be used as edge weights. |

## 4. My Thoughts

### Discussion Item 1: Change in the Co-stay Determination Method

- **Current thoughts**:

  I believe that the new method, which determines co-stays in 10-minute units, can represent the times at which co-stays occur in greater detail than the conventional six-time-period classification. Therefore, I would like to use the data created by the new method for future temporal hyper-bipartite graphs and Tucker decomposition.

- **Reason for thinking so**:

  In the old method, each stay is assigned to one of six time periods based on its midpoint time. For example, within the “daytime (10:00–15:59)” category, a co-stay around 10:00 and a co-stay immediately before 16:00 are treated as occurring in the same time period. However, the purposes of the activities and the composition of users may differ between the two, and there is a problem that heterogeneous co-stay patterns are integrated because of the long time width.

  In the new method, one day is divided into 144 10-minute slots, allowing temporal differences in co-stays to be retained in greater detail. In addition, because one long stay can be assigned to multiple time slots, detailed temporal factors can be extracted in Tucker decomposition.

#### Old Method

In the old method, the midpoint between the start and end times of each stay was used as the representative time, and one day was classified into the following six time periods based on that time.

| Time period | Time |
|---|---|
| Early morning | 4:00–6:59 |
| Morning peak | 7:00–9:59 |
| Daytime | 10:00–15:59 |
| Evening | 16:00–17:59 |
| Evening peak | 18:00–20:59 |
| Nighttime | 21:00–3:59 |

Two stays that satisfied all of the following conditions were then determined to be co-stays.

- Same day
- Same building
- Same time period
- Different users
- Actual stay periods overlap by 5 minutes or more

Therefore, in the old method, one stay is assigned only to one time period corresponding to its midpoint time.

#### New Method

In the new method, one day was divided into 144 slots at 10-minute intervals. The overlap time between each stay interval and each 10-minute slot was calculated, and the stay was assigned to the relevant slot only when the overlap was 8 minutes or more.

A co-stay was then determined when the following conditions were satisfied.

- Same day
- Same building
- Same 10-minute slot
- Each user stays in the relevant slot for 8 minutes or more
- Two or more different users are present

Therefore, in the new method, one stay can be assigned to multiple 10-minute slots. On the other hand, when the stay duration is short or when each overlap becomes less than 8 minutes as a result of crossing a 10-minute boundary, the stay is not assigned to any slot.

#### Example of Assignment to 10-minute Slots

The stay duration is **12:00–14:37**, and the inclusion condition is an overlap of **8 minutes or more** with each slot.

| 10-minute slot | Overlap time | Decision |
|---|---:|:---:|
| 12:00–12:10 | 10 minutes | Included |
| 12:10–12:20 | 10 minutes | Included |
| … | … | … |
| 14:20–14:30 | 10 minutes | Included |
| 14:30–14:40 | 7 minutes | Excluded |

In the old method, this stay is assigned to one “daytime” category based on its midpoint time. In the new method, the stay interval is expanded into multiple 10-minute slots, allowing the temporal continuity of a long stay to be retained.

### Discussion Item 2: Target Facilities and Interpretation of Tucker Decomposition Results

### 7.2 Method of Nonnegative Tucker Decomposition

#### Step 1: Definition of Modes and Discretization of Time

A three-mode tensor consisting of buildings $b\in B$, time slots $t\in T$, and users $u\in U$ was constructed.

1. The building mode targeted buildings within a 500 m radius centered on Saijo Station where co-stays were identified by the new method. The number of target buildings was $|B|=178$.
2. The time mode divided one day into 144 intervals of 10 minutes each. Therefore, $|T|=144$.
3. The user mode used the set of users who participated at least once in a co-stay under the new method. The number of target users was $|U|=884$.

#### Step 2: Assignment to 10-minute Slots

For each stay record $i=1,\ldots,N$, suppose that user $u_i$, building $b_i$, start time $s_i$, and end time $e_i$ are given.

Let the start and end times of time slot $t$ be $\tau_t^{\mathrm{start}}$ and $\tau_t^{\mathrm{end}}$, respectively. The overlap time $\Delta_{it}$ between stay $i$ and time slot $t$ is defined by the following equation.

$$
\Delta_{it}
=
\max\left(
0,\,
\min(e_i,\tau_t^{\mathrm{end}})
-
\max(s_i,\tau_t^{\mathrm{start}})
\right)
$$

A stay is assigned to the relevant slot only when a stay of 8 minutes or more is identified within the 10-minute slot. The indicator function representing a valid assignment is defined by the following equation.

$$
z_{it}
=
\mathbf{1}
\left\{
\Delta_{it}\geq 8\ \mathrm{minutes}
\right\}
$$

#### Step 3: Construction of the Three-dimensional Tensor

A three-dimensional tensor consisting of buildings, time slots, and users is defined as follows.

$$
\mathbf{X}
\in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{|B|\times|T|\times|U|}
$$

The tensor element $X_{btu}$ is defined as the number of times user $u$ was observed in building $b$ during time slot $t$, as follows.

$$
X_{btu}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\mathbf{1}\{b_i=b\}
\mathbf{1}\{u_i=u\}
z_{it},
\qquad
(b,t,u)\in B\times T\times U
$$

The set of nonzero elements $\Omega$ is defined as follows.

$$
\Omega
=
\left\{
(b,t,u)\in B\times T\times U
\mid
X_{btu}>0
\right\}
$$

#### Step 4: Nonnegative Tucker Decomposition

The factor matrices corresponding to the building, time, and user modes are defined as follows.

$$
\mathbf{A}
\in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{|B|\times R_B}
$$

$$
\mathbf{C}
\in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{|T|\times R_T}
$$

$$
\mathbf{D}
\in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{|U|\times R_U}
$$

The core tensor is defined as follows.

$$
\mathbf{G}
\in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{R_B\times R_T\times R_U}
$$

The approximate tensor $\hat{\mathbf{X}}$ of the original tensor $\mathbf{X}$ is expressed as follows.

$$
\hat{\mathbf{X}}
=
\mathbf{G}
\times_1\mathbf{A}
\times_2\mathbf{C}
\times_3\mathbf{D}
$$

Each element is expressed as follows.

$$
\hat{X}_{btu}
=
\sum_{p=1}^{R_B}
\sum_{q=1}^{R_T}
\sum_{r=1}^{R_U}
G_{pqr}
A_{bp}
C_{tq}
D_{ur}
$$

In this analysis, the following ranks were set.

$$
(R_B,R_T,R_U)=(6,6,8)
$$

#### Step 5: Optimization Problem

In Nonnegative Tucker Decomposition, nonnegative constraints are imposed on all factor matrices and the core tensor, and the error between the original tensor and the reconstructed tensor is minimized.

$$
\min_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{A},\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D}\geq0}
\left\|
\mathbf{X}
-
\mathbf{G}
\times_1\mathbf{A}
\times_2\mathbf{C}
\times_3\mathbf{D}
\right\|_F^2
$$

The meaning of each element is as follows.

- $A_{bp}$: strength with which building $b$ belongs to building component $p$
- $C_{tq}$: strength with which time slot $t$ belongs to time component $q$
- $D_{ur}$: strength with which user $u$ belongs to user component $r$
- $G_{pqr}$: strength of the combination of building component $p$, time component $q$, and user component $r$

The convergence tolerance was set as follows.

$$
\mathrm{tol}=10^{-6}
$$

Compared with CP decomposition or NTF, in which components correspond one-to-one, the ability to represent multiple combinations among building, time, and user components is an advantage.

- **Current thoughts**:

  In the current analysis, examples were identified in which stays were assigned to apartments and condominiums located near large commercial facilities or cultural facilities, resulting in excessively high factor values for those buildings. Therefore, after rechecking the building assignment method, I believe it is necessary to limit the targets to commercial facilities, cultural facilities, public facilities, and similar facilities that are expected to be used by an unspecified number of people, and conduct Tucker decomposition again.

- **Reason for thinking so**:

  In the first- and third-ranked components, “Suigun no Sato” and nearby apartments showed high building factor values. However, these are located near Halows and Sunsquare Higashihiroshima, and stays at large facilities may actually have been incorrectly assigned to nearby buildings.

  In the second-ranked component as well, the contributions of buildings near Youme Town Higashihiroshima, such as “Grandeur Shibata” and “Hiraga Mansion,” were large. If the building names are interpreted as they are, this would indicate co-stays in residential buildings. However, when positioning errors affecting nearby facilities are considered, they may represent the use of commercial facilities.

- **Alternative ideas considered**:

  - Integrate large facilities and surrounding buildings into a single facility cluster.
  - Limit the analysis targets to facilities classified as commercial, public, cultural, transportation, and similar uses.

### Discussion Item 3: Future Research Direction

- **Current thoughts**:

  I believe that it is difficult to conduct full-scale mechanism design based on individual utility using only GPS data. Therefore, I would like to proceed with research extending my undergraduate research by analyzing the influence of transportation accessibility on the formation and temporal changes of co-stay networks within business areas.

- **Reason for thinking so**:

  The current GPS data provide information such as stay locations, stay times, estimated home locations, and co-stay histories. On the other hand, private information required for mechanism design, such as location preferences, travel burdens, and intentions to interact, cannot be observed. In addition, the sample size of Higashihiroshima residents visiting Saijo Town from outside Saijo Town is limited, making it difficult to estimate responses to incentives based only on past behavioral histories.

  In my undergraduate research, I analyzed the effect of a Shinkansen extension on the number of co-stays. Using the time-specific co-stay networks being constructed in the current research would make it possible to expand the analysis beyond the total number of co-stays to include the formation of new relationships, the continuation of existing relationships, and changes in network structure. The content learned at Network Camp can also be utilized.
